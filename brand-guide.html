The wordmark

Two colourways, one accent. Everything a designer, a printer, or a journalist needs to place the Robomotion logo correctly — and the small number of things that would place it incorrectly.

Colourways

Pick by contrast, not by habit

There are exactly two versions. The wordmark switches between black and white to hold the background; the orange accent never changes.

For press: the sRGB HEX value is authoritative. The CMYK figure is a profiled conversion against FOGRA39L (ISO Coated v2), relative colorimetric — confirm it against your own press profile and ask for a proof. An uncalibrated conversion yields 0 / 60 / 100 / 2, which prints noticeably duller. No Pantone match is specified: match to a physical guide, never to a screen.

Clear space

Give it room

Keep clear on all four sides at least half the cap height of the R — that is 0.35× the logo's total height. Nothing enters that zone: no type, no rules, no image edges, no other logos.

Dashed boundary — the minimum clear space. Treat it as the logo's true edge.

Vertical measure — cap height of the R. Half of it sets the margin.

Inner outline — the artwork bounds. The masters are cropped to exactly this.

Scale

Minimum size

Below 120 px wide the orange accent thins toward invisibility and the counters begin to fill in. In print, hold to 25 mm minimum, 30 mm or more preferred.

Misuse

Don't

  • Don't rotate, outline, or re-angle any part of the lockup.
  • Don't separate the accent from the wordmark or reposition it.
  • Don't re-typeset the name — the wordmark is drawn as outlines, not live text.
  • Don't place a version on a background it can't hold — white on light, black on dark.

Every PNG has a transparent background. The white version will look blank in a file browser on a white page — that is expected, not a broken file. Both PDFs are true vector, single page, with no embedded raster.

One change from the original source file. It carried a second, mirrored accent shape at the top of the canvas set to 2% opacity — invisible on screen, and a liability in print where it can surface as a faint tint patch or a banding artifact. It has been removed. Everything else is geometrically identical to the original, verified pixel-for-pixel at 4× scale. Removing it also freed 26 units of dead space above the artwork, so these masters are cropped tight — set margins with the clear-space rule instead.
